# Academic Phrase Patterns

These are adapted functional patterns, not verbatim extracts or a verified frequency list. Select a pattern only when it serves the paragraph's task. Prefer direct affirmative statements; preserve necessary scientific conditions and limitations.

## Problem and gap

- `Accurate and efficient ... is essential for ...`
- `However, existing methods remain constrained by ... and ...`
- `When ... varies across cells or datasets, ... may fail to ... consistently.`
- `The key challenge is therefore to ... while maintaining ...`

## Method introduction

- `To address these challenges, this study develops ...`
- `The framework contains two coordinated stages: ...`
- `The proposed approach treats ... as a search variable to ...`
- `The module first ..., and then ..., thereby ...`
- `This design reduces the complexity from ... to ... while retaining ...`

## Module roles

- `... is used to preserve short-range local variations.`
- `... establishes cross-position interactions through agent-mediated aggregation and broadcasting.`
- `... further refines the fused representation over a longer temporal scale.`
- `Together, these modules form a staged local-global modeling pathway.`

## Results

- `Results show that ... maintains lower errors across ...`
- `On ..., which exhibits ..., the model tracks ... more closely.`
- `This result is consistent with the design objective of ...`
- `... achieves the lowest macro-average error across ...`
- `... achieves the lowest error on [specific cell].`
- `The ablation results separate the contribution of ... from that of ...`

## Boundaries and conclusion

- `Source-only evaluation reveals the difficulty of direct cross-dataset transfer.`
- `Few-shot adaptation reduces ... from ... to ...`
- `The remaining ... indicates limited ... under these conditions.`
- `The parameter and storage results support the potential for lightweight deployment.`
- `Future work will examine ... under ... conditions.`

## Avoid by default

- `prove`, `ensure`, `fully resolve`, `universally applicable`
- `significantly` without a statistical basis
- `ready for deployment` without hardware evidence
- repeated `It is worth noting that`, `Obviously`, or `It can be clearly seen that`
- routine `not ... but ...`, concessions, and fairness or rigor self-evaluations that add no necessary information
- appended disclaimers such as `without establishing a unique causal mechanism`; check causal strength internally and use appropriately cautious verbs in the manuscript
