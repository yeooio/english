ABSTRACT
To address the computational bottleneck of deep learning, a key artificial intelligence (AI) technology, in
resource-constrained Battery Management Systems (BMS), this paper presents a lightweight, linear-complexity
State of Health (SOH) estimation framework. First, a systematic feature selection process identifies robust
health indicators from partial voltage–time integrals, effectively reducing online extraction burdens. Subsequently, we propose SL-AgentNet (integrating Small-kernel and Large-kernel convolutions), an AI architecture
designed to resolve the quadratic overhead of traditional Transformers. By integrating the Fast Local–
Global Fusion Attention (FLFA) mechanism with diversity-enhancing modules, specifically the Fast Diversity
Feature Fusion Attention (FDFA) and Parallel Informed Activation Function (PIAF), this AI model achieves
linear complexity while capturing long-range degradation dependencies. Experiments on the Oxford, National
Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA), and Center for Advanced Life Cycle Engineering (CALCE)
datasets demonstrate superior robustness, reducing the Mean Absolute Error (MAE) by 74.78% on the Oxford
dataset against Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)-Transformer baselines. With a model size of only
40.92 Kilobytes (KB), the AI framework proves its feasibility through hardware-in-the-loop validation on a
Texas Instruments (TI) Cortex-M4 microcontroller, where a Look-Up Table (LUT) implementation maintains
accuracy loss within 0.3%, demonstrating its readiness for embedded deployment.

1. Introduction
Global energy sustainability relies heavily on critical advancements
across the broad energy storage and management spectrum. Recent
years have witnessed diversified progress in this domain. In the realm
of novel materials, significant strides have been made in stabilizing alternative chemistries, such as the interface synergistic stabilization for zinc anodes (Hu et al., 2024) and movable SiO2 reinforced
coatings (Wu et al., 2025). Simultaneously, at the grid system level,
new source-load coordinated optimization frameworks have been proposed to enhance distributed energy efficiency (Wang et al., 2025).
Concurrently with these emerging technologies, Lithium-ion batteries
(LIBs) currently remain the primary power source for Electric Vehicles
(EVs). However, as LIB technology evolves towards higher energy
densities (e.g., high-nickel cathodes, cell-to-pack (CTP) architectures),

they exhibit highly non-linear and heterogeneous degradation behaviors. These complexities challenge the fidelity of traditional physical
models, making accurate State of Health (SOH) monitoring within
resource-constrained Battery Management Systems (BMS) increasingly
challenging (Rouholamini et al., 2022; Krause et al., 2021).
Accurate estimation of SOH is crucial for mitigating range anxiety
and reducing the risk of potential failures. While direct measurement
of capacity is typically considered accurate, it requires time-consuming
full charge–discharge cycles, a requirement often impractical for online applications (Wei et al., 2018). Consequently, data-driven estimation methods that infer SOH from indirect sensor data have gained
prominence. Researchers have extensively investigated various Health
