allowing the model to adapt rapidly to local structural shifts
without the memory inertia typical of RNNs.

focuses on the spatio-temporal structural domain to capture local–
global dependencies regardless of signal magnitude. By fusing these
distinct perspectives, specifically amplitude significance and temporal
structure, the model achieves a comprehensive representation where
the strengths of one branch compensate for the limitations of the other.
This results in robustness that transcends simple summation.

5.6.4. The efficiency-accuracy trade-off
SL-AgentNet distinguishes itself through a strategic balance between
resource consumption and estimation reliability. While the CNN-LSTM
baseline offers the lowest computational cost (115 M FLOPs) and
marginally faster inference (0.74 ms), this lightweight nature compromises representation capacity, resulting in a relatively high RMSE of
0.0100. In safety-critical BMS applications, an estimation error of 1%
is frequently deemed the threshold of unreliability, potentially necessitating conservative battery utilization or obscuring rapid degradation
signals.
Conversely, SL-AgentNet exchanges a manageable increase in computational load—remaining well within the sub-millisecond safety margin of 0.77 ms—for enhanced performance. By reducing the RMSE to
0.0042, our model achieves a 58% error reduction relative to CNNLSTM. This trade-off is justifiable for modern electric vehicles: the
incremental increase in storage (40 KB vs. 15 KB) and FLOPs is negligible for automotive-grade MCUs, whereas the improved accuracy
contributes to extended battery lifespan and safer operational limits.
Thus, SL-AgentNet provides an effective solution where precision is
paramount, yet hardware constraints remain respected.

5.6.1. Experimental setup for latency measurement
Unlike theoretical FLOPs, inference latency is typically considered a
primary determinant of real-time feasibility on embedded controllers.
A standardized benchmark environment was established using a CPU
to simulate the sequential processing characteristic of BMS MCUs,
adhering to the following protocols:
• Device & Process: Measurements were executed on a CPU with
a batch size of 1 to mimic the stream-processing nature of BMS
operations. The protocol comprised 10 warm-up runs followed by
1000 active measurement cycles to promote statistical robustness;
only forward inference time was recorded, excluding data I/O
overhead.
• Input Constraints: The input dimensions were fixed at (1, 𝑁, 𝐶).
While Transformer and LSTM baselines utilized a context window
of 𝑁 = 5, SL-AgentNet employed a restricted window of 𝑁 =
4 due to the architectural configuration of the Agent mechanism. Achieving improved accuracy with this reduced receptive
field—𝑁 = 4 versus 𝑁 = 5—highlights the enhanced efficiency of
SL-AgentNet’s feature extraction capabilities.

5.6.5. Implementation challenges and hardware validation
Deploying deep learning models on embedded systems requires
addressing specific hardware limitations, particularly regarding nonlinear operations.
Floating-point unit (FPU) limitations. The proposed PIAF module utilizes heterogeneous activation functions (e.g., Tanh, GeLU) involving
exponential calculations (𝑒𝑥 ). On low-power automotive MCUs, these
operations can be computationally expensive and may induce latency
jitter.

5.6.2. Inference latency and real-time capability
Table 13 indicates that SL-AgentNet achieves a sub-millisecond inference latency of 0.7656 ms alongside a throughput of 1306 samples/s.
Furthermore, the model demonstrates the lowest latency standard deviation of 0.0907 ms among all tested architectures. This minimal variance suggests enhanced temporal stability—an important factor for supporting deterministic behavior in safety-critical Real-Time Operating
Systems (RTOS).
Although the LSTM baseline exhibits marginally lower latency (≈
0.69 ms) due to minimal recurrent overhead in short sequences, SLAgentNet performs comparably to the efficient CNN-LSTM (≈ 0.74 ms)
and outperforms Transformer-based models (≈ 1.0–1.1 ms). These results indicate that the linear-complexity FLFA module mitigates the
computational burden typically inherent in attention mechanisms.

Hardware-in-the-loop validation. To validate the feasibility of the proposed framework, we conducted deployment experiments on a TI

cycle counts. Crucially, the SOH estimation discrepancy (accuracy
